Amin Chiakha has reportedly rejected an official offer from an unnamed European club competing in the Champions League this season because he wants to remain at Rosenborg.

Algerian outlet Maghreb-Foot says it spoke directly with Chiakha's father and representative, Nabil Chiakha, about the striker's future.

According to Nabil, Amin has a clear long-term ambition to play in England or Germany. However, he recently received a formal offer from what his father describes as a respected European club participating in this season's Champions League and decided not to accept it after considering the proposal.

His father says the decision was not an isolated one. Chiakha has reportedly rejected other approaches as well because he currently wants to stay at Rosenborg, where he has settled well and feels valued.

### A significant development for Rosenborg

The situation is particularly interesting because Chiakha is still owned by F.C. Copenhagen.

The 20-year-old joined Rosenborg on loan in January, with the agreement running through the end of 2026. FCK's official announcement also confirms that Rosenborg have a purchase option included in the deal.

The size of that option has been reported differently by various outlets, while FCK have not publicly disclosed the amount. It should therefore not be treated as confirmed.

Any permanent stay at Lerkendal would still require Rosenborg to resolve the contractual situation with FCK, but Chiakha's apparent desire to remain gives RBK an encouraging position.

### Strong form in Trondheim

Chiakha has increasingly established himself as one of Rosenborg's most important attacking players.

Maghreb-Foot credits him with 10 goals in 18 appearances across competitions, while FotMob lists nine goals and two assists in 17 Eliteserien appearances.

His performances have naturally increased interest from elsewhere, but if his father's account is accurate, the most significant part of the story is not simply that European clubs are interested.

It is that Chiakha is reportedly prepared to turn down immediate opportunities elsewhere, including an offer from a Champions League participant, because he wants to continue at Rosenborg.

That does not mean a permanent transfer is agreed or guaranteed.

But with a purchase option already included in the loan agreement, strong performances on the pitch and the player himself reportedly wanting to stay, Rosenborg appear to have several important pieces in place if they decide to pursue a permanent deal.
