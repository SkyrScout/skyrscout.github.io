Moisés Paniagua should be playing football.

The 19-year-old Bolivian attacker has just completed 170 minutes for his country in the November international window, starting and playing 90 minutes against Peru before featuring for 80 minutes against Ecuador.

Yet at club level, he has effectively disappeared.

Paniagua has not played an official match for Wydad AC since 5 July and has repeatedly been left out of the squad during the new Moroccan season. That contrast is increasingly difficult to ignore: Bolivia still trusts him to start senior internationals, while one of the country's biggest young talents is losing crucial development time in Casablanca.

The reason appears to have much more to do with money and contractual chaos than football.

When Paniagua left Always Ready for Wydad at the start of 2026, the move was initially structured as a loan. His agent, Silvio Fontana, told Radio Mars in November that Wydad agreed to pay $300,000 for the loan, with an obligation to buy triggered after the player made two official appearances.

According to Fontana, that clause was activated. He said the agreement then required further payments of $850,000 in June and another $850,000 at the beginning of the current season, alongside a four-year contract for the player.

Fontana told Radio Mars that those payments were not honoured.

Bolivian outlet DIEZ reported in September that Paniagua had been omitted from Wydad's 32-man squad for the 2026/27 season and was training separately from the first team. Fontana has also claimed that the contractual situation blocked possible moves elsewhere and had already damaged the player's market value.

There then appeared to be a breakthrough.

Visión 360 reported on 6 October that Paniagua had returned to first-team training. Ten days later, the same outlet quoted Fontana as saying that an agreement had been reached and that Wydad would settle the transfer, with Paniagua expected to remain at the club until 2030.

Less than a month later, the case was back where it started.

Moroccan outlet CSport reported on 11 November that FIFA had imposed a registration ban on Wydad because the financial obligations connected to the Paniagua transfer had still not been fulfilled. According to the report, the sanction could cover up to three transfer windows unless the debt is settled.

That leaves uncomfortable questions about what exactly happened to the agreement announced in October.

Fontana also told Radio Mars that alternative solutions had been proposed. One was a free loan to Saudi club Al-Khaleej, who would have covered Paniagua's full salary and received an option to buy. According to the agent, Wydad did not provide a concrete response.

In the same interview, Fontana claimed that Wydad had previously rejected approaches for the player, including an offer worth around $4 million.

If those claims are accurate, the situation becomes even harder to understand.

A club that has failed to meet its financial commitments has simultaneously kept control of a player it is barely using, while alternatives that could have given him regular football have apparently gone nowhere.

For a 19-year-old, that matters.

Before moving to Morocco, Paniagua scored 13 goals and provided eight assists in 25 league appearances for Always Ready in 2025. He was not moving to Wydad as a finished product, but as one of Bolivia's most exciting young attacking players.

Development at that age depends on matches.

Visión 360 reported in October that members of Bolivia's national-team staff had publicly expressed concern about the lack of club minutes among several players based abroad, including Paniagua. Yet the player himself has now shown that he is available: he played 90 minutes against Peru and another 80 against Ecuador within four days, strongly suggesting that injury is not the explanation for his absence at Wydad.

The question is therefore no longer simply why Paniagua is not playing.

It is how long this situation can be allowed to continue.

Wydad may still resolve the financial dispute and reintegrate him properly. A January loan or permanent move could also offer a way out. But after months of uncertainty, contradictory announcements and missed payments, time is becoming part of the problem.

Paniagua is still only 19. His career is certainly not ruined.

But a major young talent spending month after month away from competitive club football because of a dispute he did not create is not harmless either.

For the sake of the player, something needs to change.
